01 Project · Piracicaba, Brazil

Walking Access to the SUS Primary Care Network in Piracicaba/SP

Network-based walking time to primary care units and a proposed location for a new one

Overview

The study measures walking access to primary care along the actual street network rather than with buffers. Each 2022 Census population cell is linked to the public road without crossing barriers, and travel time to the nearest unit accounts for the topography. Four in ten residents live more than 15 minutes away, concentrated in the expanded centre between traditional units. Crossing with income shows that low-income residents are, on average, closer to units — the remaining gap is small and fragmented, and it drives the simulation of a new unit. The proposed location in Vila Industrial combines a numerical criterion with a territorial check as a technical decision.

Problem

Primary care is the entry point to Brazil's public health system (SUS) and depends on proximity. Straight-line buffers understate real distance: walking routes follow the street grid, detour around rivers, highways and gated areas, and are slowed by slopes.

Objective

Estimate how many residents of the main urban area live more than 10, 15 and 20 minutes' walk from a primary care unit (UBS/USF), map that gap, cross it with income and test where a new unit would bring the most low-income residents within 15 minutes.

Methods & tools

  • Population: IBGE 2022 Census Statistical Grid — 2,542 inhabited cells, 401,137 residents.
  • Destinations: 63 primary care units from the municipal list, with CNES coordinates checked against addresses.
  • OpenStreetMap pedestrian network; 60 m/min on flat ground adjusted for slope (Tobler's function on the 30 m ANADEM terrain model, sampled every 30 m, by direction).
  • Income: average income of the household head (V06004) per census tract; low income = up to 2 minimum wages (2022).
  • Simulation of the network nodes as a hypothetical new unit, followed by a territorial check of the five best alternatives.

Results

  • 40.05%

    of residents (160,650 people) live more than 15 minutes' walk from the nearest unit; population-weighted mean of 16.17 min.

  • 13,139

    residents end up more than 15 minutes away solely because of slope along the route — a model without terrain misses these differences.

  • 16.28%

    of low-income residents are more than 15 minutes away, against 49.51% of everyone else — consistent with family health units in the periphery; the priority gap totals 18,302 people in scattered pockets.

  • 1,749

    low-income residents would come within 15 minutes with a simulated unit in Vila Industrial (−9.56% of the low-income gap; 2,274 people overall), chosen after a territorial check of five alternatives.

Limitations

  • Times are modelled along street centrelines; sidewalks and crossings were not surveyed.
  • Income is a tract-level average for household heads and does not describe individual households.
  • Physical proximity is not service coverage: capacity, staff, opening hours, land and costs were not assessed; the simulated unit is hypothetical and not a public investment recommendation.
