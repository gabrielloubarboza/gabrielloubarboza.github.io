04 Project · Seville, Spain

Territorial Inequality in Urban Bus Service Provision in Seville (Spain)

Population density, walking access and scheduled frequency of the TUSSAM network

Seville, Andalusia, Spain

Overview

The study separates three questions that are often conflated: is there a stop nearby? how many lines serve it? how often? Physical coverage is high, but accessible service varies widely: neighbourhoods such as Nervión exceed five lines, while many depend on one or two. The 33 priority sections are small pieces within dense neighbourhoods, not large service voids. In Triana and Los Remedios some residents lack a nearby stop; in Cerro-Amate almost everyone has one, but with few lines and long headways.

Problem

Seville has a dense bus network, but service is unevenly distributed. A section may have nearby stops and still few lines or long headways: a missing stop and weak service are different problems.

Objective

Identify Seville's census sections that combine high population density with relatively insufficient urban bus service, and explain what that shortfall consists of in each case.

Methods & tools

  • Three dimensions measured separately: proximity (a served stop within 400 m along the pedestrian network), line diversity and departures per hour without double-counting the same trip.
  • Official INE population by section (1 Jan 2024) and approximate residence location from the IECA 250 m grid (887 cells).
  • TUSSAM GTFS weekday schedules in three periods (07–09, 12–14, 17–19); 230,199 repeated stop passages removed.
  • OpenStreetMap pedestrian network respecting rivers, railways and motorways.
  • City-internal thresholds (quartiles) and an A–D typology: low coverage, few lines, low frequency, combined deficiencies.

Results

  • 94.36%

    of residents have a served stop within 400 m on foot (average walk of 209 m): inequality lies more in service intensity than in stop availability.

  • 3.55

    distinct lines within reach of the average resident in the morning peak; neighbourhoods such as Nervión exceed five, while many depend on one or two.

  • −15.5%

    scheduled accessible departures in the afternoon compared with the morning (29.8 → 25.2 per hour for the average resident): the afternoon keeps its lines but loses frequency.

  • 33

    priority sections (41,208 residents, 6.0% of the population) combine high density with at least one deficiency; 22 of the 23 with low morning frequency keep it in all three periods.

Limitations

  • Measures GTFS scheduled service, not real punctuality or ridership; 77% of stop times are interpolated (a ±2 min sensitivity changes mean frequency by less than 0.5%).
  • Only TUSSAM urban buses: metro, tram, commuter rail and metropolitan buses were not included.
  • Thresholds are relative and internal to Seville, not technical or legal standards; some sections with severe deficiencies and mid-range density fall outside the 33.
