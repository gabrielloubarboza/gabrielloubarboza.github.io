05 Project · Curitiba, Brazil

Deficit on the Map

Intra-urban analysis of Curitiba's housing deficit in 2024

Independent portfolio material based on IPPUC (2026), Déficit Habitacional em Curitiba 2024: Análise Intraurbana (Housing Deficit in Curitiba 2024: Intra-urban Analysis). It is not an official publication. The study is a collective work coordinated by Bruna Griguol, Érika Haruno Hayashida and Mônica Máximo da Silva.

Overview

IPPUC brought the housing deficit measure inside the city by adapting the national method to CadÚnico. This case study follows the path from administrative register to map — record selection, anonymisation, geocoding, cleaning, hierarchical classification and spatial distribution — and clearly separates institutional responsibility from individual contribution. Results show that rent dominates the deficit and that people in deficit are, comparatively, poorer, more often women, more often Black and younger than Curitiba's average.

Problem

The housing deficit is usually measured for the country, states and municipalities using sample surveys. Without an intra-urban reading, planners cannot tell where to prioritise investment or how to reach households in need.

Objective

Show where and how the housing deficit manifests within Curitiba by adapting the João Pinheiro Foundation (FJP) method to a continuously updated administrative register (CadÚnico) and mapping results by administrative region and neighbourhood.

Methods & tools

  • Data: Curitiba's CadÚnico (Brazil's social programmes register) extracted in June 2024, updated records only; anonymised under Brazil's data protection law (LGPD).
  • Address geocoding on the official street register, with manual review of cases below the matching threshold.
  • Outlier review using an adapted interquartile range, case by case.
  • Four sub-components classified hierarchically (each family counted once): improvised dwelling, rustic dwelling, excessive rent burden and cohabiting families.
  • Indicator extraction and spatial distribution by administrative region and neighbourhood; concentration maps.

  • 293,457people in those families
  • 10administrative regions
  • 75neighbourhoods mapped

Results

  • 34,967

    families or family units in housing deficit — 27.2% of the CadÚnico universe analysed; 84,743 people.

  • 88.4%

    of the deficit is excessive rent burden; precarious housing accounts for 9.6% and cohabitation for 2.1%.

  • 64%

    of the deficit lies in five administrative regions — CIC, Cajuru, Bairro Novo, Tatuquara and Boqueirão — concentrated in the southern, south-eastern and south-western periphery, with pockets in the centre.

  • 75

    neighbourhoods show excessive rent burden; the deficit is concentrated in informal settlements but is not limited to them.

Limitations

  • CadÚnico covers registered — mostly the most vulnerable — households: the adaptation aims to map the deficit, not to replace the original method's absolute count.
  • Results refer to June 2024; later resettlements and land regularisation may change the picture.
  • The report does not publish geocoding parameters (except the threshold of 80), outlier thresholds or the number of records excluded at each step.
